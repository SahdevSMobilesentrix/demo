How This Bot Trades — In Plain English

This page explains exactly what the bot watches, when it enters, when it exits, and what safety rules stop it from blowing up. No jargon — just rules.

1. What is this bot doing?

Every minute, the bot looks at the NIFTY option chain (Calls and Puts around the at-the-money strike) and asks one question: “Are big players writing Puts or writing Calls right now?”

  • Heavy Put writing + Call covering → big players expect price to go up → buy a Call (CE).
  • Heavy Call writing + Put covering → big players expect price to go down → buy a Put (PE).
  • If the picture is mixed or quiet, the bot does nothing.

It is a paper-trading bot. No real orders are placed. P&L is simulated using live premiums and realistic broker charges (brokerage, STT, exchange txn, SEBI, stamp duty, GST).

2. The numbers on the Live tab — what each one means

Market BiasThe bot's verdict: Strong Bullish / Bullish / Neutral / Bearish / Strong Bearish. Computed from Signal + price action.
Trade ActionWhatever the bot did on this tick: OPEN_LONG_CE@…, EXIT_…, or blank.

3. When does the bot ENTER a trade?

All four of these must be true at the same time. If even one fails, no trade.

  1. Strong bias only. Bias must be Strong Bullish (for a Call) or Strong Bearish (for a Put). Mild "Bullish/Bearish" is rejected — too noisy.
  2. 3 ticks of confirmation. The last 3 ticks (≈ 3 minutes) must all agree on the same Strong bias. One green tick is not enough — the bot waits for the move to hold.
  3. Price must have broken the strike. For a Call, NIFTY spot must be above the at-the-money strike for at least 2 ticks. For a Put, spot must be below the strike. No buying into resistance.
  4. Which strike is bought: Once all gates pass, the bot buys the 1-step in-the-money (ITM) option, not ATM. For a Call, that's the strike 50 points below spot (e.g. ATM 24200 → buy 24150 CE). For a Put, the strike 50 points above spot (e.g. ATM 24200 → buy 24250 PE). ITM has higher delta (~0.6 vs 0.5), less theta-as-percent, and intrinsic value cushions small adverse moves — better risk-reward than buying naked ATM premium.
  5. Momentum must still be moving in our direction. Latest spot must be making progress (higher for Calls, lower for Puts) compared to the start of the 3-tick window. No entering on a stall.

When any of these checks fail, the Server Log will say something like skip LONG_CE — no sustained breakout of 24200. That's the bot doing its job.

4. When does the bot EXIT?

A trade closes the moment ANY of these is true:

  • Target hit (+15%). Premium has risen 15% above what we paid → take profit.
  • Stop hit (−10%). Premium has dropped 10% below what we paid → cut the loss.
  • Bias flips. If we are long a Call and bias turns Bearish (or long a Put and bias turns Bullish) → exit immediately.
  • Bias decays. If 2 consecutive ticks after entry lose the original Strong bias (drop to Neutral / mild) → exit. Don't sit in a fading move.
  • Session end. Any open position is force-closed at the last available premium when the run window ends.

5. Daily safety rules (so the bot can't blow up)

  • Trades per day cap. Default 1 trade/day. Configurable in the Live tab. Once hit, no more entries until tomorrow.
  • First-loss lockout. If your first trade of the day closes at a loss, the bot is done for the day — no second trade. (Cuts revenge-trading.)
  • Daily loss limit: 10%. If realised P&L for the day reaches −10% of capital, the bot halts until next IST day.
  • Cooldown after exit. A short pause after every exit before considering a new entry — stops back-to-back fakes.
  • Session phase. No entries before 09:15 IST or after 15:30 IST. No entries during pre-open or post-close.
  • Insufficient balance check. If the trade cost exceeds the running balance, the bot refuses to enter and shows a banner.

6. How money is tracked

  • Starting capital is used only the very first time. After that, the running balance is the source of truth — wins add, losses subtract.
  • Each closed trade is enriched with realistic charges (brokerage, STT, exchange txn, SEBI fees, stamp duty, GST) on the Trade History tab.
  • Net P&L shown anywhere = Gross P&L − all charges. That's what actually hits the wallet.
  • The running balance is persisted on the server, so even if you restart, yesterday's results carry over.

7. The Trade History tab — what the analytics mean

  • Win Rate — % of trades closed with a profit (after charges).
  • Profit Factor — Total winnings ÷ Total losses. Above 1 = profitable. Above 1.5 is healthy. Above 2 is strong.
  • Risk / Reward — Average win ÷ Average loss. Tells you if winning trades pay more than losing trades cost.
  • Expectancy / Trade — Average rupees made per trade. Positive = the system is making money on average.
  • Max Drawdown — Biggest peak-to-trough fall in equity across the full history. The "worst pain" the system put you through.
  • Max Win / Loss Streak — Longest run of consecutive wins or losses. Good for sizing your nerves.

8. What this bot does NOT do

  • It does not place real orders. Everything is simulated.
  • It does not use price-only indicators (no RSI, no MACD, no candlestick patterns). Decisions are driven by OI structure + simple price-vs-VWAP confirmation.
  • It does not chase moves. If the entry rules don't all line up, it simply waits.
  • It does not hold overnight. Open positions are flat by session end.
  • It does not add to losers, average down, or pyramid winners.

9. Tunable knobs (Live tab)

  • Lots — Position size. 1 lot = 75 NIFTY units. Changing this updates the live session immediately.
  • Starting Capital — Used only on the first run. Ignored afterwards.
  • Trades / Day — Hard cap. Default 1. Can be changed live without restarting.
